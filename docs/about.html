How the data is built, and what it means

What each grouping is for, where it comes from, how often it changes, and where the edges are. Short version: every list comes straight from its publisher, and every row says whether it is a UN Member State.

The question this project exists for

Why Africa has 54 countries, or 60

The UN uses two different kinds of country list, for two different jobs.

54UN Member States in Africa. The political list: who sits in the General Assembly.
60Countries and areas in the M49 region “Africa”. The statistical list: everywhere data is reported for.
48World Bank “Sub-Saharan Africa”. An analytical region that leaves North Africa out.

UN membership is decided by the General Assembly under the UN Charter. There are 193 Member States, and two non-member observer States: the Holy See and the State of Palestine.

UN M49 is the UN Statistics Division's Standard country or area codes for statistical use. It covers every place for which statistics are compiled, including territories that are not States. In Africa that adds six areas: British Indian Ocean Territory, French Southern Territories, Mayotte, Réunion, Saint Helena and Western Sahara. Worldwide, M49 lists 248 countries and areas.

Both are correct. They answer different questions. Trouble starts when a report filters on “region = Africa” in an M49 table and calls the result “African countries”. Every file here carries un_status (member_state, observer_state or non_member) and a plain is_un_member flag, so the choice is explicit.

The African Union has 55 members, including the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic, which is not a UN Member State. This project publishes UN, World Bank and OECD groupings only; it does not publish African Union membership.

UN regional groups

Member States organise themselves into five regional groups for elections and the allocation of seats in UN bodies. They are a list of States by definition, so they never include territories. un_regional_group holds the group used for electoral purposes:

CodeGroupMember States
AGAfrican Group54
APGAsia-Pacific Group54, plus Türkiye (see below)
EEGEastern European Group23
GRULACLatin American and Caribbean Group33
WEOGWestern European and Others Group29, including Türkiye and the United States
  • Türkiye participates fully in both the Asia-Pacific Group and WEOG; for electoral purposes it counts in WEOG only. The long membership table lists it in both, the un_regional_group column says WEOG.
  • United States belongs to no regional group. It attends WEOG meetings as an observer and is treated as a WEOG member for electoral purposes.
  • Israel has been a full WEOG member since 2000 (temporary basis), renewed permanently in 2004.

Source: UN Department for General Assembly and Conference Management, Regional groups of Member States.

Choosing a grouping

Which list should I use?

If you are…Use
Counting or listing countries, States, Member Statesis_un_member = true, then any region
Following UN statistical practice, e.g. SDG reportingregion, sub_region, sdg_region, all areas
Writing about elections, seats or negotiating blocs in UN bodiesun_regional_group
Working with World Bank datawb_region, wb_income_group
Working on development financeis_oda_eligible, oda_recipient_group
Reporting on countries in special situationsis_ldc, is_lldc, is_sids, is_fcs
Splitting Africa into its five familiar partsanalysis_region: the M49 intermediate region where there is one, otherwise the sub-region

Automation

How a refresh works

A GitHub Actions workflow runs scripts/update_data.py at 05:17 and 17:17 UTC every weekday and at 09:42 UTC on Sundays. When it finishes, a second workflow republishes this site, so the files and pages always match.

  1. FetchUN M49 (all six language tabs), the UN SDG API, the World Bank country API, the current World Bank FCV list (PDF) and the latest OECD DAC recipient list (CSV).
  2. MatchM49 is the backbone. Other sources join on ISO3 where they publish it, otherwise on a normalised name with a small alias table. Names that do not match are written to unmapped_external_names.csv; today there are none.
  3. CheckThe run stops if the UN membership list is not 193 + 2, the regional groups do not add up, or OECD membership is not 38.
  4. PublishCSV with a single header row, JSON for the web, SHA-256 checksums in run_manifest.json, a dated snapshot in data/history/ and a plain-language changelog in data/changelog/.

Read before you rely on it

Limitations

  • Not an official product. Country Classification Commons is an independent open-source project. It is not issued or endorsed by the United Nations, the World Bank or the OECD. For anything formal, check the publisher's own list.
  • Upstream changes break parsers. For FY27 the World Bank replaced its FCS list file with a “Public FCV List” that no longer splits conflict from institutional fragility, so fcs_category is empty from FY27. The run flags such breaks instead of publishing stale data silently.
  • Names follow M49. Short names are the UN's, for example Türkiye, Côte d’Ivoire and Naoero (Nauru). Other publishers' names are matched to them, not shown.
  • World Bank columns are filled for 215 of the 248 areas. Areas the World Bank does not classify have them empty, and economies it lists without an M49 code, such as Kosovo, are not included.
  • Timing differs by publisher. World Bank income groups change on 1 July, the FCV list at the start of the World Bank fiscal year, the OECD DAC list every three years, LDC status on graduation dates set by the General Assembly.
  • Coordinates are World Bank capital-city points, useful for dot maps, not for boundaries.
